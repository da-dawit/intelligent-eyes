Intelligent Eyes: Teaching Frozen ViT Models Where to Look in Video

Dawit Chun
Taejae University · DA411 Computational Mathematics, Fall 2026

Abstract

Frozen vision encoders such as CLIP and DINOv2 see every frame shrunk to 224 pixels, so a small animal in a camera-trap clip may cover only a few patches. Zooming into every part of every frame fixes this, at ten times the cost. We give a frozen encoder eyes: it glances at each frame once, predicts what each of the 9T possible 2× crops would tell it, and spends a budget of looks on the crops with the highest expected information gain, in closed form. Votes from all observations are combined by Bayes' rule with a fitted correlation R, so repeated evidence is not counted twice.

As first proposed, every look lowered accuracy. We traced the cause: two thirds of crops show only background, and a crop classifier trained on clip labels learned that scenery means species. With crop labels from animal boxes (training only), gated fusion, a shared crop error in R and a two-branch information gain, two looks now add __GAINS__ points over glancing on iWildCam test cameras with four frozen encoders and land on the animal in __HIT__% of cases; with the stopping rule, about one look per clip matches zooming everywhere.

Method

1. Glance. Every frame is encoded once at 224 px; a linear classifier turns it into a vote ℓ(y) = log q(y | frame) − log π(y). 2. Predict each look. From the glance's own patch tokens over a crop, linear classifiers predict whether the crop holds the animal and what its vote would be. The information a look is expected to give is EIG = P(animal) · ½(Varp[m] + v tr F), the closed form for the animal branch; a look that shows only background changes nothing. 3. Look. The best crop is encoded at 2×. Its vote is log(q(y | c)/π(y) + q(empty | c)), which is about 0 for a background crop. 4. Update. All votes are combined as p(y) ∝ π(y) exp(w⊤ℓ(y)) with w = R−11 and R = Tρ ⊗ Aλ + τ 1c1c⊤, fitted on held-out cameras. Steps 2–4 repeat until the budget is spent or the best EIG falls below ε.

What went wrong first

Animal boxes from MegaDetector, used here only as a measuring stick, showed the problem. A crop classifier trained with the clip's species on every crop was right on only 43% of the crops that do hold the animal (a), and the proposed eyes looked at the animal no more often than chance (b). Bayes' rule made it worse: it read a background crop's near-zero vote as evidence against the glance. Training crops with an empty class, leaving background looks out of the combination, adding a shared crop error to R, and splitting the information gain into an animal and a background branch fixed both: crop accuracy rose to 86%, and the final eyes find the animal in __HIT__% of their looks.
